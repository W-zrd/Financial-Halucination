## Executive Summary

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (`TSM`, NYQ) should be owned, but gradually. That convergence itself is informative. The aggressive analyst won the directional argument on evidence; the conservative analyst won the discipline argument on structure; the neutral analyst's synthesis (40–50% initial, verified before the last dollar) is the operating plan that survived every attack from both sides.

Action guidance, expressed against your own standard single-name allocation since we cannot see your book:

Accumulate on weakness or neutrality, never into record-high strength. The one price-behavior datapoint in the record (the 09/22 fade on thinning volume against a record Taiex close) suggests supply exists at these levels, meaning patience costs little.

Consider defined-risk insurance on the initial tranche through the summit if priced fairly — window insurance, not tail elimination. Record a loss limit at fill as a commitment device, honestly labeled as psychology rather than gap protection.
5. **If you already hold `TSM` oversized** (grown past 8–10% of your portfolio through appreciation), trim into strength toward standard sizing — concentration management is separate from the directional call.

Why Overweight rather than Buy: the debate proved the business case decisively but also proved the price case unverified. Every plan at the table ended in owning `TSM`; the number was set by what nobody could verify — a quote, a multiple, and the caller's book. That evidentiary gap, plus a live dated geopolitical catalyst and a next margin datapoint that points down, caps conviction at gradual accumulation rather than full deployment.

## Investment Thesis

**The evidence that decided it:**

The bull case rests on delivered, checkable facts, not projections — a distinction the aggressive analyst deployed effectively against the conservative analyst's Cisco-in-2000 analogy. Gross margin expanded from 59% to 68% year-over-year at 72.5% foundry share. A14 tape-outs are ahead of schedule toward 2028. A flagship Qualcomm part is already shipping on 2nm — a launch customer locked in before the node matured. Capital commitments run at $31B of quarterly scale ($15.59B capex up 42% quarter-over-quarter, plus $16B in equipment approvals). As the aggressive analyst framed it, this is capex built against contracts, not hope — and companies do not accelerate capacity spending into the front edge of a demand cliff. The record's sentiment data supports entry conditions rather than contradicting them: a 6.2 mild-bullish score, thin chatter, 69k watchers described as a sleeping giant, retail doing weekly DCA accumulation, and a ~15% ADR premium versus 40% for a comparable peer ADR. That is not a euphoric-top signature; the conservative analyst conceded this point explicitly, withdrawing "fragile" as his characterization.

The bear case landed three real hits that cap the rating, and each survived cross-examination:

1. **The record contains no valuation anchor.** No P/E, no forward multiple, no revenue growth figures — the aggressive analyst's "no plausible multiple is a sell" line was correctly prosecuted by both colleagues as the most dangerous sentence of the debate, the exact sentiment written about every franchise at every peak. His own repeated offer to verify the multiple before the final tranche was, in effect, a concession.
2. **The dominant downside is a gap.** The Trump-Xi summit is a live, dated catalyst in the record, and a Taiwan-origin tariff shock would reprice before quarterly-information tripwires could read it. The conservative analyst's best sentence — "for gap risk, the sizing is the hedge" — was adopted by the entire table, and his sequencing argument won: a permanent position must be sized for the day the hedges expire, because that day is certain while the benign summit is not. A cheap expiring put covers a window, not a standing geopolitical condition.
3. **The next datapoint points down.** The margin guide steps from 68% to ~66%. Both sides agree the cause is 2nm ramp dilution — a pattern that has historically resolved into margin recovery — and the neutral analyst's framing is the correct one: one guided step down is dilution; two consecutive guides down is a trend. This makes the guide the single most important upcoming check, not a thesis break.

The neutral analyst's structural refinements were adopted because they survived both sides: the conditional dip rule (a dip with quiet tripwires and no adverse news is accumulation; a headline-driven dip is not free), the cooling rule with the summit carve-out, the pre-committed verification thresholds written before the quote is pulled, and the caller-side concentration check. Two further standing risks entered the record late and belong on the monitoring list: the ADR premium as an independent loss mechanism that can compress, and the New Taiwan dollar earnings versus New York listing currency mismatch.

**Why not Hold or Sell:** conflict between analysts is not grounds for Hold when one side's evidence is categorically stronger. The bull side's case is built on delivered operational facts; the bear side's counter-case consists largely of hypotheticals (an unnamed hyperscaler decision, a 2030 Intel allocation, an unimposed tariff regime) plus the genuine but non-directional discipline constraints above. Both opposing analysts ended their closing arguments agreeing `TSM` should be owned — the conservative analyst explicitly: "Buy TSM — I have never disputed the direction."

**What would change this call:**

- **Upgrade to Buy:** the verification gate passing (forward multiple in line with peer mega-cap semis relative to growth), a benign summit resolution, and one confirmed margin quarter at ≥66% with ramp-attributed dilution — at that point the position earns full standard allocation.
- **Downgrade to Underweight:** any two tripwires firing — most plausibly, margin guidance tracking toward ~60% (structural rather than ramp pressure) combined with either A14 slippage or disclosed leading-edge allocation leakage to Intel/Samsung. A single trip pauses all adding and triggers reassessment.
- **Reduce outright:** two consecutive margin guides pointing down (the "one is dilution, two is a trend" rule), or verification revealing peak-cycle pricing that the record's evidence cannot support.

**Explicitly missing information:** the record contains no current quote, no P/E or EV/EBITDA, no price target, no balance sheet detail, and no revenue growth figures — only margin levels, market share, capex, and qualitative sentiment data, plus a single retail reference price (442.14) of unknown valuation context. This is the single largest gap in the debate and the primary reason the correct rating is Overweight rather than Buy. Source current multiples independently before completing the final tranche, and anchor all subsequent levels to your actual fill price.
