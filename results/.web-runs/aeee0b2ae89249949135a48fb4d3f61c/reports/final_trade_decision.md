**Investment Thesis**

The three seats converged on the core action and disagreed only about the retained position, which is itself informative: after six rounds, nobody produced an argument for buying here. The debate's structure — unanimous on the trim, split on how to express the remainder — tells me the residual disagreement is about *how much risk to keep*, not about direction. That is a sizing problem, and the honest resolution is the smaller position.

**What decided it:**

1. **The fundamental drift is unidirectional and not yet decelerating.** Gross margin fell four consecutive quarters (55.6% → 55.3% → 54.6% → 53.8% → 53.1%); operating margin fell in an accelerating path (27.5% → 21.8% → 16.3%); operating cash flow down 65% and free cash flow down 81% in the latest quarter. The aggressive analyst's central fact set — inventory up 28% into a declining revenue line, receivables up 63%, capital returns at 126% of trailing FCF, dividends in the June quarter roughly twice that quarter's FCF — was never rebutted. The conservative analyst accepted these figures and argued only that they are *priced*; he did not dispute that they are deteriorating. That is the strongest single point in the debate, and it belongs to the bear side.

2. **The bull-side risk controls were shown to be structurally weak, and the conservative conceded the key point.** The conservative analyst argued the 0.80x net debt to EBITDA fortress protects the position. The neutral analyst dismantled it — "net debt to EBITDA tells you the company survives; it tells you nothing about whether the stock gaps down eight percent on a buyback cut" — and **the conservative explicitly conceded this**: "net debt to EBITDA protects solvency, not drawdown." That concession is decisive for position sizing. The balance sheet is why we can hold a *smaller* position patiently; it is not a reason to hold a large one through a binary catalyst. The conservative then walked back to leaning on the fortress anyway, which weakens his own case rather than restoring it.

3. **The aggressive analyst's probability and payoff claims failed on their own terms, but his falsifier critique survived.** The neutral caught him quoting 2-to-1 off a 4.3% stop while quoting his hit rate off a 1.9% invalidation — two different trades — and the aggressive **conceded it**: "He's right... those don't belong to the same trade." His restated 65–70% claim anchored on a close below 194.53, which the neutral and conservative both correctly identified as a move inside the noise band on an 8.65 ATR (3.7% down, on a name that moved 5.8% in a single session on 2026-09-18). So I do not credit his directional conviction or his payoff math. **What survives is the structural point the neutral conceded on his behalf**: the 184.97 and 169.64 levels are terrain for the retained position's next decision, not return objectives. That is a real contribution, but it is a *monitoring* contribution, not a reason to hold more.

4. **The conservative's "already priced" argument cuts against his own sizing.** He argues the deterioration is in the 202 price because QCOM is a 206-billion-dollar company with sell-side coverage. The neutral's reply is correct and unanswered: if the deterioration is priced, the Apple renewal through 2027 and the Snapdragon design wins are priced too, leaving no edge in either direction — which argues for the *smallest* defensible position, not a specific half-benchmark weight. He also concedes the 10 EMA conditional-tranche refinement and adopts the neutral's mechanical volume version, further collapsing the distance between his position and a smaller one.

5. **The gap-event point is the strongest argument on the table and it favors the smallest position.** The conservative's observation that neither a stop (210.62) nor a put works across a gap is correct: if the print delivers a 53% margin with the buyback held, the stock gaps through the ceiling; if it delivers a 51% margin with inventory building, it gaps through the 191.02 trigger. Both the aggressive's stop and the neutral's mechanical breakdown rule assume continuous pricing in the one event where continuity fails. Sizing — not rule-construction — is the only control that works. Given a stock that can move 5.8% in a session and 13.6% in five, and given a catalyst that adjudicates gross margin, buyback pace, and a 28% inventory build simultaneously, the half-benchmark ceiling is a ceiling and should be treated as one.

6. **The earnings-quality problem is real and cuts against anchoring on reported numbers.** A ~$6B tax charge against a ~$5B tax benefit, ~$972M of non-operating securities gains (~9% of trailing net income), and $3.2B of stock-based comp explaining why $9.25B of buybacks shrank the share count only 2.7%. This means the market is pricing QCOM on a narrative, not clean cash flow, and the neutral is right that this makes the November reaction function genuinely two-sided. But two-sided reaction functions argue for *less* exposure, not for a directional bet in either direction. The aggressive's attempt to convert this into a one-way ratchet — "narrative stocks gap down" — was correctly called out by the conservative as one-sided, since the same mechanism gaps it up on a clean print.

**What I am explicitly not crediting:** the aggressive analyst's "crowded, complacent, fully-positioned crowd." The neutral and conservative both correctly dismantled it — the 202.5-strike call gains (up 267% and 322%) are expiry mechanics per the sentiment report itself, and seven labelled StockTwits posts with low confidence and one source returning nothing is not a crowd. The aggressive ultimately conceded the point: "seven labeled StockTwits posts is not a crowd, and I shouldn't lean on the 86/14 split." I also decline both sides' inferences about the undisclosed Apple renewal terms — the dataset contains no renewal terms, no guidance, no earnings-call commentary, and no segment detail. Both the bull's "favorable negotiation" inference and the bear's "15-month deferral" inference are equally unsupported.

**What the bull side genuinely establishes, and why this is Underweight and not Sell:** the technical trend is intact (full MA stack, MACD 8.12 above a 5.99 signal with a widening histogram, VWMA at 184.97 rising beneath price), the balance sheet is genuinely strong (0.80x net leverage, ~14.9x interest coverage, ~10.4B TTM FCF, 2.02x current ratio, ~6.4% total shareholder yield), the Apple licensing contract runs through 2027 and removes a renewal cliff, Microsoft's Surface lineup is fully on Snapdragon, and the macro source identifies QCOM as the relative safe haven within semis. That is a franchise worth owning at a smaller size and a better price — not one to bet against. The insider data (CEO sold 10,000 shares at $195 four days before the analysis; COO sold in eight of nine months; zero open-market buys anywhere in the dataset, including at the ~$147 August low) is a mild-to-moderate negative that argues for less size; it is pre-scheduled activity, not proof, and I weight it accordingly, as the neutral recommended.
