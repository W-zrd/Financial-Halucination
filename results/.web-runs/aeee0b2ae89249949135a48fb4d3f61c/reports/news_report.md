## 3. QCOM-Specific Assessment

**Fundamental framework (based on the resolved identity; no fresh vendor data available):**
- **QTL (licensing):** contractual royalty streams on essentially every 5G handset shipped. High-margin, cash-generative, and largely insulated from a cyclical demand wobble — but structurally exposed to handset unit softness, Apple's modem in-sourcing trajectory, and regulatory/antitrust scrutiny across the EU, China, and Korea.
- **QCT (chipsets):** Snapdragon mobile SoCs plus a growing diversification push into automotive, IoT, and PC (Arm-based compute). Diversification reduces single-end-market risk but the segment remains tied to the premium Android handset cycle and to Chinese OEM demand.
- **China exposure is the key idiosyncratic risk:** revenue concentration with Chinese OEMs plus any change in export-control or tariff regime is the single largest swing factor. **Note: the news tools returned nothing on this — I could not verify the current state of US–China tech trade policy, so I am flagging it as an unquantified risk, not asserting a view.**

**Interest-rate sensitivity:** with the 10Y at 5.18% and rising, QCOM's valuation multiple faces direct compression pressure. A high-single-digit-FCF-yield-style licensing business is relatively better positioned than a pure high-multiple growth chip story, but it is still a long-duration equity in a rising real-rate world.
