**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**

The bear case wins this debate, but not for the reason the bear thinks. The bear did *not* prove AMD is a bad business — both sides agree it is an excellent one: revenue +50% YoY, operating income swinging from $(134)M to $1,990M in four quarters, gross margin re-based from 39.8% to ~53–54%, $8.8B net cash, $8.4B TTM FCF, 6% debt/equity. That is not in dispute and is not the deciding factor.

What decided it is the **price, and the bull's own framing of it.** Three arguments carry the most weight:

1. **The bull conceded the entry.** Across six rounds the bull's actionable instruction was consistently "don't chase $630," "scale in on weakness," "initiate a *starter* position," "size so a 20% drawdown is a 3–5% portfolio hit," "invalidation at the 50-SMA ($504.71)." That is a wait-and-trim posture dressed in bullish language. When asked the question the bull himself accepted as the crux — *is $630.63 a good price?* — his answer was no. You cannot recommend a position you simultaneously tell people not to pay for.

2. **The reward/risk is genuinely unfavorable, and it is unfavorable on the bull's own numbers.** Upside anchor: the street-high BofA target at $720, ~+14%. Downside to the bull's own stated invalidation line: $504.71, ~−20%. The bull's attempt to call this a "strawman" (distinguishing a target from an invalidation level) is fair in principle but does not rescue the trade — if the honest posture is "own a small position because a 20% drawdown is plausible," the correct action for new capital is to *wait*, not to initiate.

3. **The margin acceleration story has stalled, and that stall is the entire bull thesis.** This is the sharpest point either side made. Gross margin went 39.8% → 51.7% → 54.3% → 52.8% → 53.8%. The 1,400bp re-base *already happened and is already priced*. Three quarters without a new high, while the bull asks the room to extrapolate a vertical line from a slope that has flattened. The bull's "step-function then consolidation at a new base" defense is real — but it is a level argument, not a rate argument, and rates are what get extrapolated into multiples. Combined with the fact that AMD is priced at the peer group's *highest* multiple on the *lowest* operating margin (Trefis), this is a pure multiple story. Multiple stories are the first thing to compress when the discount rate moves.

The bear's supporting arguments are **directionally supportive but individually weaker**, and I am not weighting them heavily:
- *Insider selling:* The bear's opportunistic-vs-routine framing is legitimate, and the C-suite's clustered selling at $500+ with zero offsetting open-market buys is a real negative tell. But insider selling has genuinely modest predictive power over multi-year horizons, and this signal has fired repeatedly against a 291% move. It supports caution; it does not carry the call.
- *Macro:* Real and adverse — Fed flat at 3.63%, CPI +3.05%, core PCE 2.92%, 10Y at 5.18% (+100bp y/y), 2s10s flattening. The bear is right that the long end discounts high-multiple equities regardless of whether the Fed hikes. But the bear's implicit 2022-repeat (a ~55% drawdown) is not evidenced; that required 425bp of actual tightening plus an earnings contraction. This is a headwind, not a thesis-breaker.
- *Technical:* Both sides are reading the same tape. RSI 73, +28% in nine sessions, ATR ~$25 (3.9%), price pressing the upper Bollinger band, first real support 7% below spot. The bull's "light-volume pullback = consolidation" and the bear's "light-volume pullback = thin order book" are both plausible; neither side proved theirs. Net: tactically extended, ambiguous signal. Supports not adding, not a sell call.
- *Quality of earnings:* The bear overstated this by comparing pretax to net to manufacture a 26% haircut. Cleaned properly, EPS is ~$1.02 vs. $0.54 — still ~89% growth. This argument *failed* on inspection.

**What this is not:** It is not a Sell. The company's fundamental trajectory is accelerating on every line, the balance sheet is a fortress in exactly the regime where leverage gets punished, and the bear produced no evidence of an actual deterioration — no operating-margin contraction, no datacenter deceleration, no hyperscaler capex cut, no design loss. Selling outright would require the fundamentals to be deteriorating.

**What this is:** The company is excellent and the entry is bad. That combination maps to Underweight — reduce exposure into strength rather than exit, and do not commit new capital at $630.

**One explicit gap:** Neither party had reliable point-in-time valuation data — the fundamentals report withheld multiples, and both sides ended up constructing FCF multiples from a market cap figure. Do not treat the "119x FCF" figure as verified; it was assembled by the bear, contested by the bull, and I could not independently confirm it from the materials provided. The Underweight call rests on the margin-rate stall, the reward/risk asymmetry, and the extended technical setup — none of which depend on that multiple.

**Strategic Actions**

Sized against a standard single-name allocation:

1. **Trim existing positions into strength. Reduce to roughly 60–70% of intended full position size.** Use the $690–720 zone (approaching the street-high target) as the primary trim window. If the position was accumulated below $300, this is profit-taking into a parabolic extension, not a thesis exit — the core stays.

2. **Do not initiate new capital at current levels.** No starter position, no "small bite." The bull's own logic — a 20% drawdown is plausible and should be sized for — argues for waiting, not for pre-positioning.

3. **Set a staged re-entry ladder, contingent on the fundamental monitors holding:**
   - First tranche (one third of intended position): on a pullback to the 10-EMA zone around $585, **and only if** the decline comes on declining volume without a fundamental catalyst.
   - Second tranche (another third): toward the $505–530 zone approaching the 50-SMA.
   - Final third: only after a confirmed higher low above the 50-SMA with gross margin rate re-expanding.
   - If price reaches the low-$500s, re-underwrite before buying — do not buy mechanically. A 20% drawdown from a parabolic top is exactly when the fundamental monitors matter most.

4. **Invalidation trigger — exit the remaining core:** A close below $504.71 (50-SMA) **on rising volume.** That is the bull's own line and it is a reasonable one. Above it, the secular bid is intact; below it on volume, the structure has broken and the position should be closed rather than averaged.

5. **Fundamental monitors that would upgrade this to Buy or Overweight:**
   - Gross margin **rate** printing a new high above 54.3% — this is the single most important one.
   - Datacenter revenue growth holding above 20% YoY.
   - Accounts receivable growth decelerating toward revenue growth (currently flagged at AR +20.6% QoQ vs. revenue +12.5% QoQ — a real warning, not yet a thesis-breaker).
   - CapEx conversion evidence — the tripled CapEx ($808M in Q2 alone) producing measurable revenue return.
   - Any sign of insider open-market **buying**.

6. **Fundamental monitors that would downgrade to Sell:**
   - Two consecutive quarters of operating margin contraction with flat-to-lower revenue.
   - A hyperscaler capex cut or a major accelerator design loss.
   - Sustained 10Y above 5.25% alongside a datacenter growth deceleration.

7. **Position sizing discipline:** Size the *eventual* full position so that a 20% drawdown to the 50-SMA represents no more than a 3–5% hit to the overall portfolio. This is the bull's own sizing guidance and it is correct — it is also, notably, a sizing rule that assumes the entry will be bad, which is why the current entry does not merit full size.

**Bottom line:** Hold the core, trim into strength, do not add at $630.63, and keep a defined ladder to re-engage on weakness with the 50-SMA as the hard invalidation line. The business earns the position; the price does not earn the entry.
